How we calculate

How CarDep works out what a car costs per month, where the numbers come from and how the value grade is set.

The monthly cost is everything you pay for the car while you own it, divided by the number of months: depreciation, fuel or electricity, servicing, tyres and repairs, insurance and road tax. Loan interest is not included. The model pages assume 5 years of ownership and 12,500 km a year. In the calculator you can change everything.

Depreciation
Purchase price minus the estimated residual value. How fast each model loses value is worked out from what the cars cost in ads today, model year by model year. The first year has the biggest drop, then the rate slows. The residual value also depends on how far you drive: for every 10,000 km more than a typical car of the same age when you sell, the car is worth about 2 percent less, and the other way round. That is how much ad prices differ for the same model and model year. The residual value is what the car costs in ads, roughly what you get if you sell privately. If you trade the car in at a dealer you get less, because the dealer has to make money selling it on: under Advanced options you can choose trade-in, and we then count 85 percent of the ad price. That is our estimate, based on dealers often marking up 15–19 percent (CarUp, Sep 2025) and buying in for 75–90 percent of what they sell for (BilKoll, Aug 2026). We do not count haggling, since you probably haggle about as much when you buy as the buyer does when you sell.
Used cars
Used prices are based mainly on ads on Blocket, Sweden's largest marketplace. In October 2026 we went through about 41,700 Swedish ads for every model year on AutoUncle, which shows which site each ad is on. About half, 20,900 ads, are on Blocket. For each model year we use the median price of the Blocket ads when there are at least five, which is the case for 73 percent of the model years. Otherwise we use the other Swedish ads from dealers and sites such as Bytbil and Wayke, or prices from BilKoll collected in August–October 2026. Where a model year still has too few Swedish ads, we use ads from Finland, and for electric cars also from Denmark. Their prices are converted to the Swedish price level using twelve models sold in all three countries. Danish petrol and diesel cars are left out, because the registration tax makes them too expensive to compare. The purchase price of a used car starts from the ads for that model year: we compare each ad with the new price of the same engine and trim and convert the median to the version you choose. Where a model year has few ads, a depreciation curve for the whole model is weighed in, and Blocket ads count for more than others. The price never goes below what the cheaper ads with the same or more mileage cost (the cheapest fifth for the engine, or tenth for the whole model year), so you should be able to find a car at that price. Versions that hardly appear in a model year's ads are not picked automatically. Mileage is the median in the ads for that model year. Where a model year has few ads, the typical mileage for the model and age is weighed in, and an older model year always has covered more than a newer one. The price is adjusted for the difference from the advertised cars' mileage. Ad prices are asking prices, and the final price may be lower. Servicing and repairs get more expensive with age and once the warranty has run out. Malus tax is only paid for the years left of the car's first three. For models no longer made, we use the last new price.
New cars
New cars use what Swedish dealers advertise them for. For each model we compare the price in ads for new cars in October 2026, mainly on Blocket, with the list price including VAT of the same version in the Swedish Tax Agency's new car price list of June 2026. The more ads there are, the more they weigh against the list price: with n ads the median of the ads gets the weight n/(n+4), so eight ads already count for two thirds. This applies to every version of the model. With fewer than three ads for new cars, we use the list price, and we never use more than the list price, since ads above it usually carry extra equipment. A used model year always costs at least 3 percent less than a new car and at least 2 percent less per year than a newer model year. The residual value is worked out from the ad prices, just as for used cars.
Fuel
Distance × real-world consumption × price. The default of 12,500 km per year is close to the Swedish average, which was 12,430 km in 2025 according to Trafikanalys. We convert the official consumption to real-world consumption. Petrol and hybrid cars use 21 percent and diesel cars 17 percent more than the WLTP figure. That is the average for cars registered in 2022 in European Commission data from the cars' own fuel consumption meters (petrol 21.1 and diesel 17.1 percent). Hybrids without a charging cable are counted as petrol cars there. For older cars with NEDC figures the gap is 9 to 40 percent depending on model year according to the ICCT. For electric cars we add 25 percent to WLTP, counting the electricity from the socket including charging losses. In TNO's measurements of 45,000 electric cars in the Netherlands the gap was 22 percent in 2024, and a Swedish winter uses more than a Dutch one. Plug-in hybrids use on average 3.5 times as much petrol as in the test according to the European Commission, because they are charged less often than the test assumes. We use 2.5 times as much petrol and 20 percent less electricity than the test, for a car charged at home. Privately owned plug-in hybrids are charged more often than company cars: in the ICCT and Fraunhofer study private plug-in hybrids in Norway used about twice the official figure and in Germany about 2.5 times.
Vehicle tax
Swedish vehicle tax, based on CO₂ emissions. For the first three years a malus applies: SEK 107 per gram between 75 and 125 g/km and SEK 132 per gram above 125 g/km, plus a base amount of SEK 360. After that, SEK 22 per gram above 111 g/km. Diesel cars pay a fuel surcharge (SEK 13.52 per gram) and an environmental surcharge (SEK 250). For diesel cars first registered before July 2018, the base amount and CO₂ amount are instead multiplied by 2.37, and the environmental surcharge is SEK 500 for cars from before 2008. Electric cars and plug-in hybrids pay SEK 360.
Interest on the money
Left out unless you choose it. Under Advanced options you can enter an interest rate. We then charge interest on what the car is worth, year by year: each year the average of its value at the start and end of the year times the rate. With a car loan this is roughly the interest you pay, if the loan follows the car's value. Paying cash, it is what the money could have earned in a savings account or in funds. An example: a car bought for SEK 300,000 that is worth SEK 180,000 after three years costs about SEK 800 a month in interest at 4 percent. Interest is included in running costs and in the grade. The lease-or-buy comparison always uses its own interest rate.
Insurance
Estimated comprehensive insurance per model for a driver aged 31–65. Pricier versions cost more to insure, and the premium drops as the car loses value. But it never drops below the cost of a part-comprehensive policy (57 % of the comprehensive premium for a new car of the same model) as long as the car is worth at least SEK 40,000. Below SEK 20,000 we assume roughly third-party only, with a smooth transition in between. This follows BilKoll's advice (part-comprehensive for cars worth SEK 30,000–150,000, third-party only below SEK 30,000) and their examples, where part-comprehensive costs 53–60 % of comprehensive in the same class. The share is an average and an estimate. The levels roughly match BilKoll's examples for a 35-year-old with maximum no-claims bonus (Aug 2026), for example about SEK 7,500 a year for a Golf or V60 and SEK 9,000 for an XC60 or RAV4. Under Advanced options you can choose the driver's age. We then scale the premium by the average premium per age group in Zmarta's review of about 35,000 comprehensive policies (Mar 2023–Feb 2025): 18–20 years 2.07 times, 21–25 years 1.64, 26–30 years 1.21 and over 65 0.92 times the premium for 31–65. These are averages across all cars in each group. Young drivers often drive cheaper cars, so for the same car the difference may be larger. Under Advanced options you can also choose where you live. In Compricer's review of comprehensive policies per municipality (2025), all of the ten most expensive municipalities are in the Stockholm area, SEK 7,537–8,711 a year, and the ten cheapest are small towns, SEK 4,005–4,438 a year. The national average was about SEK 6,250 (Dagens PS, Feb 2026). So we use 1.25 times the premium in the Stockholm area and 0.68 times in small towns and the countryside. This is an estimate: the average in a municipality also depends on which cars and drivers live there, and prices can differ a lot between neighbourhoods in the same city. Gothenburg and Malmö have no figure of their own, so Sweden on average fits best there for now. Your bonus and the insurer also affect the premium, but we leave those out.
Servicing and repairs
Servicing is an estimated yearly amount that rises 3 percent for each year the car gets older, up to 15 years of age. The amount is roughly one service a year. If you drive further in a year than the brand's service interval, the car needs more services, so we scale servicing up in step with the distance (at most three times as much). The intervals for petrol, diesel and hybrids: Toyota 15,000 km or 1 year, Kia, Hyundai, Ford, Mazda and Nissan 20,000 km or 1 year, Peugeot and Mercedes-Benz 25,000 km or 1 year, Volvo, Renault and Dacia 30,000 km or 1 year, BMW and the Volkswagen group (VW, Audi, Skoda, Seat, Cupra) 30,000 km or 2 years with flexible servicing (LongLife), whichever comes first (NGS Bilservice, August 2026, and Serviceboken, March 2026). Where the sources give a range we use the longest interval. It is an estimate: what each extra visit costs depends on whether it is a minor or a major service. Electric cars are usually serviced every other year whatever the distance, and for other brands we have not found the intervals, so nothing changes there. For the Volkswagen Group's electric cars (VW ID.3, ID.4, ID.5, ID.7 and ID. Buzz, Skoda Enyaq and Elroq, Cupra Born) we assume about SEK 1,200–1,500 a year for servicing. The ID.4 and Enyaq cost about SEK 1,300 a year according to both KVD and Autoexperten (May 2026, cars from 2021–2022) and Vi Bilägare's new-car tests (January 2024, three years or 60,000 km). For the other models it is an estimate. Tyres are included: a set for an ordinary family car costs about SEK 7,000 fitted and lasts about 45,000 km, roughly SEK 0.15 per km. Bigger and more expensive cars have dearer tyres (SUVs about 20 percent more, sports cars 45 percent), electric cars wear tyres about 25 percent faster, and even low-mileage drivers replace their summer and winter sets at least every 10 years. Seasonal tyre changes at a garage are not included. The roadworthiness test is included too: at 3 and 5 years and then every 14 months, about SEK 450 plus a re-test for the roughly 23 percent that fail. For Kia the amount is based on Kia's own service agreement prices (October 2026, kia.com). On top of that come expected repairs once the warranty has run out. The warranty ends after the model's number of years or once the car passes the brand's mileage limit, whichever comes first, for example 7 years or 150,000 km for Kia and MG, 6 years or 150,000 km for BYD, and Toyota Relax up to 10 years or 200,000 km if the car is serviced at Toyota (BilKoll, November 2026). So if you drive a lot, the warranty ends sooner. First, the brand's average repair cost according to RepairPal, rising with the car's age at the same rate as the share of cars with faults in the TÜV Report. Second, known faults for that specific model and engine, mainly from BilKoll's buying guides, such as the timing belt on PureTech engines, DSG gearboxes and the angle gear on older all-wheel-drive Volvos. Each fault counts as the risk that it happens while you own the car times what the repair costs, spread out per month. The risks are our estimates. Electric cars get half as much in other repairs, since they break down less often according to the ADAC breakdown statistics, but we add the risk of charging faults and of the battery needing repair or replacement (see Battery ageing).
When does a car stop getting cheaper?

On average a car stops getting cheaper to own at about 10 years. From then on repairs and servicing (SEK 1,222 a month) cost more than the car loses in value (SEK 1,202), and the total cost barely moves, around SEK 3,722 a month and SEK 3,527 at 19 years: what you save on lower depreciation, the rising repairs eat up. So on average the car does not get more expensive per month, but it no longer gets cheaper either. Median of 148 models sold new, bought new and driven 12,500 km a year.

Battery ageing
The battery in an electric car or plug-in hybrid loses capacity with age and mileage. By our calculation an electric car with a liquid-cooled battery has about 85 percent left after 8 years and 120,000 km. That matches the average of just under 2 percent a year in Geotab's measurements of more than 10,000 electric cars. Batteries without liquid cooling, as in the Nissan Leaf, VW e-Golf, Renault Zoe and Dacia Spring, age 1.3–1.7 times as fast. Tesla batteries and BYD's LFP batteries age a little more slowly. The range drops at the same rate as the capacity, and a worn battery uses a little more electricity. For a used car we show the estimated range today, both by WLTP and in real-world driving, where it is about 20 percent shorter. Older model years are calculated with the battery they had then, for example 22 or 41 kWh in an older Renault Zoe. The cost includes the risk that the battery needs repair or replacement once the battery warranty has run out. The warranty usually covers 8 years or 160,000 km and also covers capacity below about 70 percent. The risk of replacement rises when the capacity drops below 80 percent, and the risk of repair rises with age. A replacement costs about SEK 2,500 per kWh plus labour, so about SEK 110,000 for a 40 kWh Nissan Leaf. A plug-in hybrid with an older battery covers less of its driving on electricity and uses more petrol. The risks are our estimates.
Value grade
The A–E grade weighs the monthly cost against what you get. A car gets 0–5 points each for quality, design, comfort, luxury, safety, space and performance, so at most 35 points. The monthly cost gives one cost point for every 10 percent it is above SEK 1,600. What you get minus the cost points gives the grade, and A is the best value. Quality is based on reliability in the TÜV Report 2026 and the ADAC breakdown statistics 2025 where the model is included, otherwise on our estimate. It drops as the car gets older, since the share of cars with major defects at inspection rises from about 10 percent at 5 years to 33 percent at 15 years according to the TÜV Report. Safety is the Euro NCAP star rating (3 points if the car has not been tested), minus one point for cars from before 2016 and two points for cars from before 2010, which lack modern safety systems. Luxury is the same level as on the slider. Design and comfort are our judgement based on car reviews, and winners of World Car Design of the Year get top marks for design. Sporty versions get lower comfort but higher performance. Space is based on boot volume and performance on acceleration. The Best value sort follows the points.
Luxury and sportiness
Every version gets a luxury level and a sportiness level from 1 to 5. The luxury level is based on brand, model and trim level, measured against today's cars: after three years it drops a tenth of a step a year, at most one and a half steps, since equipment and technology age. Sportiness is based on acceleration, body type and brand, with a little extra for sporty versions such as GTI, AMG, M and RS. Extremely sporty in practice takes a real sports car or one of the very fastest versions, while hot hatches such as the Golf GTI and BMW M135 count as very sporty. When you choose a level, we show the cars that reach it and calculate with the cheapest version of each car that does. Cars that don't reach the level in any version aren't shown.
Fuel prices
Petrol and diesel are the big chains' list prices. The temporary cut in the carbon dioxide tax on petrol and diesel, SEK 2.40 per litre before VAT or about SEK 3 with VAT, applies from 1 July to 30 November 2026 according to the Swedish Tax Agency, and the 2027 budget does not extend it. A price from before 1 December is therefore about SEK 3 per litre lower than what you will pay for almost all of the time you own the car. We show today's price but calculate with today's price plus SEK 3 per litre. Prices you enter yourself are used as they are. We calculate the home-charging price as an average over the ownership, because the car is charged all year and the spot price varies a lot between months. In SE3 that is SEK 1.88/kWh incl. VAT: the spot price averaged 93.7 öre/kWh over November 2025–October 2026 (boio.se), the retailer's margin about 14 öre (variable price, typically 10–16 öre according to boio.se), the grid's variable transmission fee about 35 öre (the average of Ellevio, 26 öre, and Vattenfall Eldistribution, 44.5 öre) and energy tax 45 öre (Skatteverket). The margin and grid fee are estimates and differ between companies. Fixed fees are left out, since charging does not change them, and so is a power-based fee, which most large grid companies removed or paused in 2026. Prices update automatically once a day. If you live in another electricity area you can pick it under Advanced options. We then add the difference in spot price compared with SE3, averaged over November 2025–October 2026 incl. VAT according to boio.se: 38 öre/kWh less in SE1, 36 öre less in SE2 and 28 öre more in SE4. Grid fees differ more between grid companies than between areas, so we treat them as equal. You can enter your own prices under Change prices. Electric cars are not only charged at home: 87 percent of EV owners mostly charge at home according to M Sverige's big EV survey 2026, but they too fast-charge on longer trips. We estimate that 15 percent of the electricity is charged in public, at about SEK 5.70 per kWh without a subscription (OKQ8, Circle K, Recharge and Ionity charge SEK 5.49–6.09 per kWh according to hurbra.se, August 2026). You can change the share under Advanced options. Plug-in hybrids are counted as charged at home.
Lease or buy
For cars that are commonly leased privately in Sweden we compare the brand's own private lease offer with buying the same version new and selling it when the lease ends, usually after 36 months. Leasing: the payment at your distance (the brand's price for more distance where given, otherwise an estimate), set-up and invoice fees and any higher first payment spread per month, and servicing if it isn't included. Buying: the depreciation from the list price of the same version (or the ad price for new cars, if lower) to the residual value in ads, interest on the money (5 percent unless you change it) and servicing. The residual value is adjusted by 2 percent per 10,000 km you drive more or less than the model typically covers over the same years; that is how much ad prices for the same model and model year differed in October 2026. Beyond the oldest model year in the ads, the curve is extended with at least 8 percent depreciation a year, since older cars in the ads lose 35–40 percent over four years. Fuel and tax are the same, and so is insurance unless the leasing company requires its own, like Peugeot. The offers were read on the brands' websites on 26–27 October 2026 and may have changed since.
